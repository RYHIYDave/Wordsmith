Wordsmith sounds real where the world is and retro where the game speaks. Hits crack and land with weight; loot and level-ups bleep like an old console; a word rings like a bell, then sparkles. The music is Dream Thrash made with synths: light and airy while you explore, punched way up in a fight.

## Pillars

Five rules sit above all the others. When two rules clash, these win, the higher one first. That order is the director's suggestion.

1. **Cool and dangerous.** Dark places, but a fight is a thrill: confident, punchy, lively. Never gloomy for the sake of it.
2. **Real world, retro game.** The world sounds like the things in it: steel, bone, stone, fire. The game itself speaks like an old console: loot, menus and level-ups bleep.
3. **Everything has weight.** A hit is a sharp crack, then a heavy thud. Light for small hits, heavy for big ones.
4. **Words ring out.** A word is where the two voices meet. When one drops, or works, you know it by ear.
5. **Massive and airborne.** The music is Dream Thrash made with synths: weightless while you explore, massive when a fight starts.

**Never:** cartoony (no boings, slide-whistles or comedy sounds); harsh or piercing (nothing shrill or painful, however loud, even on a phone's small speaker).

**Not ruled out, by your choice:** wet hits (you chose “A little (Recommended)”) and a wall of noise (you chose “Glorious chaos”).

**Whose sound it sits between:** Dead Cells and Diablo / Path of Exile for the fight and the loot; Astronoid for the music. They are for feel only; nothing is copied.

- **Real means larger than life** (director): like the thing itself, with extra punch. It is still made in code, with no recordings.
- **Retro means old-console bleeps:** short, bright and tuneful. Which old console is still to find, by ear.

## The fight

A hit that lands is a sharp crack, then weight. Everything else in a fight serves that.

1. **A crack, then weight.** Every hit has a sharp crack you can't miss and a heavy thud behind it. Light for small hits, heavy for big ones.
2. **What is hit decides the sound** (director). Bone cracks and clatters, iron rings, flesh thuds. It follows the art rule that what flies off a hit is the monster's.
3. **A little wet.** A small wet edge when a hit draws blood: a troll, a cultist. Bones only crack and clatter.
4. **The sound follows the weapon** (director). A sword is steel and weight, a bow is string and wind, a staff or wand crackles with power, whoever holds it. The swipe move is the hero's own: the leap, the roll, the warp.
5. **Magic is real power.** Fire roars, ice cracks, lightning snaps, and raw magic crackles and hums like live current. It does not bleep. You were told this in round 2 and let it stand.
6. **The freeze cuts the sound.** A heavy blow freezes the picture for a tenth of a second. In that instant everything drops away, and the blow lands in the gap.
7. **Glorious chaos.** In a big fight the hits pile up into a roar, and that roar is the reward for being strong. Early on you hear every hit. It is the same climb as “Tense early, godlike late”.
8. **Huge, never harsh.** The roar may be as big as it likes. It is never shrill or painful.

## Monsters

Monsters have voices, true to what each one is, and their big attacks warn you by ear.

1. **Voices true to each.** Bones clack and rattle, bats screech, trolls grunt and bellow, cultists hiss and chant, the Warden roars.
2. **Each monster its own** (director). Its blow, its being hit, its death and its voice are its own. No monster's attack borrows a hero's sound, as they all do today.
3. **By size** (director). Small monsters sound brittle and light, and are fun to break. Big ones sound slow, low and heavy, and you hear them coming. It follows the art rule: small ones fun to smash, big ones menacing.
4. **Every big attack warns by ear.** A slam, a charge, a boss's blow: each has a wind-up sound of its own, heard even in the roar. You can dodge by ear.
5. **Deaths are true to what died** (director). Bones scatter and clatter, a cultist's robe sighs down empty, a troll goes down heavy.
6. **A screech is never piercing** (director). A bat is thin and quick, never painful: your own never.
7. **A sound brief with every new monster** (director). When a new monster is designed, one more question goes to you: what does it sound like?

## Your hero

You always know by ear when your hero is hit, and when their life is low.

1. **Hit: a hard thud and a short grunt.** Bigger hits, bigger sounds. The grunt is wordless, and you hear it even in the roar.
2. **Low life: a heartbeat, and the world dulls.** A slow heartbeat comes in and everything else goes a little muffled until you heal.
3. **No voice in an attack.** A hero's voice is heard only when hurt, when falling, and when saying a line. Attacks are the weapon's and the magic's own sound.
4. **Falling** (director). When a hero falls, the fight and the music drain away to one low sound, then quiet. It is the same under YOU DIED and YOU FELL.

## Words

A word is the one thing that sounds like both the world and the game. When one drops, it is the biggest moment by ear.

1. **A word found: a deep bell, then a retro run.** One low, ringing strike you feel, then a rising run of old-console notes. The fight dips under it for a moment. Nothing else in the game sounds like it.
2. **A word at work: the real thing, with a small shine.** Flame roars, Frost cracks like ice. On top sits a short bright sparkle in that word's own note: the sound of a word at work, as its glow is to the eye.
3. **A note for every word.** Each word has a note of its own, as it has a colour of its own. Thirteen words today; every new word gets its sound with its look.
4. **In front and behind** (director). In front, the word sounds on the hit. Behind, it sounds on what the attack leaves: burning ground hisses on, frost creaks.
5. **Stacked words add up, the first leads** (director). With several words on one attack you hear each, the first one loudest.
6. **The wordsmith's own moments** (director). A word set in a slot, burned into gear, forged, or laid on the gate sounds like a smaller cousin of the bell and the run.
7. **The words win** (director). When a word drops, nothing covers it, as nothing covers it on the screen.

## Loot and big moments

You know what dropped by its sound alone, and the big moments are a proud retro fanfare.

1. **Rarity by ear.** White: one soft blip. Blue: two notes. Yellow: a bright run. A word tops them all.
2. **Gold has a jingle of its own.**
3. **Heard as it drops** (director). The rarity sound plays when the thing falls, so you know before you look. Picking it up is a small, soft tick.
4. **Unique items** (director). When they come, they get a sound of their own, above yellow and below a word.
5. **Level up, a new move opening, a boss falling:** a short, proud old-console fanfare. The music and the fight duck under it for a second.
6. **Small wins stay small** (director). A flask refilled, a chest opened, a door unsealed: short and quiet, so the fanfare stays special.

## Music

The music is a try at Dream Thrash, made with synths: light and airy while you explore, punched way up in a fight.

(The doc's picture, "Dream always. Thrash for the fight.", in words: three stretches of time side by
side, Exploring, A fight, Room clear. A band labelled "Dream half: light and airy" runs under all
three. A second band, "Thrash half", "racing drums, fast riffs", sits under A fight alone. Below
them, "Boss fights, to try: blows land on the beat": a row of evenly spaced lines, each a beat, with
a diamond, a blow, on some of them.)

The dream half plays the whole time. The thrash half is added for a fight and taken away after it.

1. **Dream Thrash.** Your words: “seamlessly fuse the absolute aggression of extreme metal with the weightless euphoria of indie pop and shoegaze, creating a sound that feels simultaneously massive and entirely airborne”.
2. **Made with synths.** Your words: “I was thinking more synth.  Light and airy in towns or exploring, punched way up during fights.” Synth is the one kind of music that code makes for real. The thrash half is synths too, so it will sound like synths playing metal, not like a recorded band.
3. **The measure is Astronoid's sound:** racing drums and fast, bright riffs under high, floating, layered melodies. Joyful, not grim. It is for feel only: every note is the game's own.
4. **Two halves** (how the director reads rounds 5 and 6 together). The dream half is the light, airy music of town and exploring, and it never stops. The thrash half punches in on top of it when monsters wake, and falls away when the room is clear.
5. **Music everywhere, each its own.** A theme on the start screen, music in town, each land's own music in its dungeons, and a piece for bosses. On 4 Oct you asked for “distinct music for each dungeon type”.
6. **One theme, then mood.** A Wordsmith tune you'd know anywhere: on the start screen, in town and in the big moments. Dungeons are mostly mood and rhythm.
7. **Bosses on the beat, to try.** Your idea of 9 Oct, 13:53: “Like get the bosses to attack on beat to the music?” In a boss fight the boss's blows land on the beat, and his wind-up sound rises into it. It needs a small change to the boss's rules, which is the main chat's to make.
8. **The music gives way** (director). It ducks under a word's bell and under a fanfare, and it never covers a warning.
9. **It is a try.** You said “I’d like to try”. The first piece is a try-out, tuned by ear, and this section follows what you say then.

## Voices

Heroes and townsfolk speak in a wordless mumble shaped like speech, each in a voice of their own.

1. **A better mumble.** Made in code, a voice cannot say real words. A line is heard as babble shaped like speech, and its words are on the screen.
2. **A voice each.** The warrior “very deep and gruff”; the ranger hushed, “a hunter in woods that doesnt want to spook anything”; the mage plummy.
3. **One voice each, no button.** The warrior and the ranger have men's voices, the mage a woman's. The VOICE button leaves the class cards.
4. **When a voice is heard:** when a hero is hurt, when they fall, and when they say a line. Never in an attack.
5. **The town's four people** (director). The armourer, the mystic, the wordsmith and the stranger get the same kind of mumble, each a voice of their own. Today they borrow the heroes'.
6. **An accent stays in the words** (director). A mumble has no accent, so the mage's “british accent” lives in her lines on the screen.
7. **Lines stay rare** (director). A line now and then, as today.

## Places

A place is alive by ear, but always under the fight and the music.

1. **Alive, but under everything.** Fires crackle as you pass, water drips, the gate hums; in town the hammer rings. All of it sits under the fight and the music.
2. **Footsteps.** Your own steps are soft on stone, and you hear a troll's tread coming. Packs of small monsters do not patter (that last part is the director's).
3. **Each land its own air** (director). A land's sounds change with its look, as its colours do.
4. **Iron and stone stay real** (director). Doors, gates, levers and traps sound like what they are.
5. **From where it happens** (director). A sound comes a little from the left or the right, and is quieter far away. On a phone's speaker this hardly shows; it is for headphones.

## The mix

Three things are always heard, whatever else is going on: a warning, your own hero being hit, and a word dropping.

1. **What cuts through.** These are your answers; their order is the director's. Everything else gives way to them.
    1. A big attack's warning.
    2. Your own hero being hit, and the heartbeat at low life.
    3. A word dropping.
    4. The fanfare of a level, a new move or a boss falling.
2. **Tuned for the phone's own speaker first.** It has no deep bass, so weight comes from the crack and the body of a sound. Then it is checked for headphones.
3. **Never harsh or piercing,** however loud.
4. **Never the same twice** (director). Every repeat is a little different, so a hundred sword hits never grate.
5. **Never by ear alone** (director). Everything you need is also on the screen: the pose, the red circle, the life bar. The game plays fine with the sound off.
6. **Loud things are rare** (director). Small hits are small so that big ones feel big, and the loudest sounds are the rarest.

## Menus and settings

Menus speak retro, softly and seldom. The retro is your answer; soft and seldom is the director's.

1. **Menus bleep.** A press, a page turned, a thing bought or equipped: short old-console sounds, in the same voice as the loot.
2. **Soft and few** (director). One quiet blip for a press and a lower one when something is refused. Nothing sounds on every small move. It matches how the menus look: “Clean with a touch”.
3. **Two volumes** (director). The game gets a volume for music and one for sounds, beside the one switch it has today: SOUND: ON or OFF. The menus are the main chat's to change.

## How sound is made and approved

A sample you can play, your yes, then one chat puts it in.

1. **Samples first.** Nothing new is heard in the game until you have listened to a sample of it and said yes. Until then it waits behind a switch that is off.
2. **A page with play buttons.** Samples come on a page for your phone, every sound with a button, old beside new. No films.
3. **Questions as pop-ups.** Every question comes with options, the recommended one first, and your own words always possible.
4. **One chat puts it in.** The sound chat works on branches of its own. Only the main chat puts sound into the game and publishes.
5. **Made in code.** No sound files: every sound and every note of music is a recipe in code.
6. **Original only.** Nothing is copied from a game, a film or a band. Dead Cells, Diablo / Path of Exile and Astronoid are for feel only.
7. **Your ears are the test.** The sound chat cannot hear. It checks each sound by measuring it and by looking at a picture of it. What you say after listening is the real test.
8. **Your words, exactly.** When a rule quotes you, the quote is exact.
9. **Numbers checked.** Every number here is checked against the code.
