# The warped wordsmith's fight: its flow (DRAFT, for his yes)

His words, 14 Nov 2026, after the last of the melds: “The problem with just doing every move before figuring out the fight is I
can’t get a flow for the thing.  I cont see the timing of what’s going to happen.  Are these different attacks going to overlap?
How many are you going to have to be aware of at any time.  There are things that are stationary that you need to be aware of,
things that are coming at you and you need to dodge, and direct damage that you’re just going to have to take as a trade off to do
damage, and open spots in the fight to pour on damage”. Asked what this chat should do next once the boss was on the ship list,
his pick: “Plan the fight's flow (Recommended)”. His rulebook's boss rule stands: long fights from what you dodge, not from more
life; each phase harder than the last, something always coming at you.

## The four kinds

- DODGE: coming at you; get out of its way (its warning first, in the bosses' ground-warning colour).
- FLOOR: stationary; it stays a while and shapes where you can stand.
- TAKE IT: damage you cannot dodge; you take it as the price of hitting him.
- OPENING: he cannot hurt you for a moment; pour on damage.

## How many at once

Never more than TWO things to watch at once, plus what is left lying on the floor: one thing he (or the boar) is doing, and one
thing his words are doing. Every attack's warning shows before it can hurt.

## Phase 1, in the saddle (full life to half): a loop of about 30 s

| s | The boar (body) | His word (on top) | Kind |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–9 | Charge, three runs, wheeling round between | Poison: spore traps laid over the floor at 2 s, there till the end of the loop | DODGE + FLOOR |
| 9–12 | WINDED: it stands panting after its third run (NEW: a panting stand) | nothing | OPENING |
| 12–14 | Gore, if you are close | nothing | DODGE |
| 14–18.5 | Rear-up stomp at 15 s, its rings | Lightning: the orb overhead, its small zaps finding you wherever you are; its big bursts dodged | TAKE IT + DODGE |
| 19–23.5 | stands | Flame: meteors over the room, each marked; their craters burn on | DODGE + FLOOR |
| 24–27.5 | stands | Power: the hammer crushing down three times on your shadow | DODGE |
| 27.5–31 | stands | Frost: the ice spikes | DODGE |

The order is shuffled each loop but for the charge, which always opens it, and the winded stand, which always follows it.

## Between: the boar's death (5.4 s)

The boar killed mid-charge; he is thrown clear, its power streaming into him, his shade rising. Nothing hurts you; he cannot be
hit (his line, “Rest, old friend. Your fury lives in me.”). A breath before the worst of it.

## Phase 2, on foot (half life to none)

Every meld: he writes one word and his shade another (1.3 s: he is busy writing, a short opening), they meld, the slam.

THE SLAM'S RING: every meld's slam throws out a small ring you cannot dodge (TAKE IT, small): the steady price of the phase.

THE MELDS BY KIND (their lengths from the slam):

| Meld | Kind | Lasts |
|---|---|---|
| Eruption | DODGE (vents blowing in a line) | 5 s |
| Hailstorm | DODGE (stones falling, marked) | 5.4 s |
| Thunder | DODGE (seven bolts locking on, the clap knocks back) | 9.6 s |
| Avalanche | DODGE (get to the strip) | 7.2 s |
| Contagion | DODGE (through its front to burned ground) | 7.4 s |
| Scald | DODGE (two beams) + FLOOR (the steam round him) | 9.5 s |
| Blight | FLOOR (the trees stand as walls) | 7 s |
| Fumes | FLOOR (the clouds) then DODGE (they blow in turn) | 7.8 s |
| Wildfire | FLOOR (the web) then DODGE (it detonates) | 7.6 s |
| Frostbite | TAKE IT (the gale) + OPENING (the eye: go in and hit him) | 6.9 s |

HALF TO A QUARTER: one meld at a time, a DODGE and a FLOOR meld by turns; after each, he stands spent 3 s, his shade dimmed
(OPENING); Frostbite every fourth.

A QUARTER TO NONE: no rest between: the next meld's writing begins while the last one's floor still stands (its trees, its clouds,
its steam): one thing coming at you, one thing on the floor, at once. Frostbite stays the one opening.

His death when his life runs out, whatever he is doing.
