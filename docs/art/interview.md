# The art rulebook: the interview (NOT IN THE GAME)

Branch `art/rulebook`, from the art chat. Nothing here changes the game. This file is the record of
how the rulebook is being found: every question put to the owner, his answers in his exact words,
and how each was read. The rulebook itself will be `docs/art/RULEBOOK.md` on this branch once he is
happy with it.

## What he asked (8 Oct 2026, 07:19, to the art chat)

"i feel like i know what i want, but im having trouble decribing it.  i want to create a set of
parameters and rules for creating assets, art, and animations.  but i cant just spit them out so
we need to figure them out.  I want you to play the art director of the video and ask me questions
until we can get a ruleset down that i am happy with."

## Round 1 (asked before 07:30; the questions' exact wording was not kept)

Four questions: the feel; how much the library-dream story should show in the art; the
proportions of heroes and monsters (the first option: heroes stylized, monsters natural); and what
the look must never be. His answers:

- **Feel:** "Cool" and "Fun".
- **The dream:** "I think we’re going to move away from that story.  I’m keeping the words, that’s
  the main mechanic, but I’m scrapping the library dream thing.  So not at all would be my answer"
- **Proportions:** "I like the first option but we’re talking about walking skeletons so how
  believable of a creature is that?"
- **Never:** "Cute or kiddy" and "Plastic 3D". (Gore and busy screens were on the list and he did
  not pick them.)

**How they were read, and told to him at 07:36:**

1. Cool and fun: confident and a bit dangerous, but lively; never gloomy for the sake of it.
2. The dream is gone, the words stay. Nothing in the art points to a library, a dream or
   detention. The book-themed floors are asked about in round 2.
3. Heroes stylized, monsters natural. "Natural" means TRUE TO WHAT IT IS, not real: bones where
   bones go, joints that bend only as joints bend, the walk of a heavy, loose pile of bones (his
   own "plodding and brittle"). The heroes are the ones allowed to be stylish.
4. Never cute or kiddy (no big eyes, round toy shapes or candy colours) and never plastic 3D (no
   smooth, shiny computer-render look). Gore and busy screens are not ruled out: asked, not
   assumed.

## Round 2 (asked 07:37, answered 07:47)

The questions, as put to him:

1. "Which of these games look closest to the Wordsmith in your head? Pick any, or name others."
   Hades ("Painted, comic-book heroes with dark ink edges, strong colour, big glowing effects."),
   Diablo / Path of Exile ("Gritty, realistic gothic. Muted colour, grime, blood, lots of
   detail."), Dead Cells ("3D models turned into pixel art. Bright colour in dark places, fast
   fluid moves."), Hyper Light Drifter ("Pixel art in neon pinks and teals, slim cloaked figures,
   few details, lots of mood.").
2. "Where should the colour live?" In the glows ("Stone and shadow in deep, cool tones. Strong
   colour only where something glows: fire, magic, eyes, loot."), Each place its own ("A dark
   base, but every area has a bold palette of its own: a green crypt, a red forge, an ice cave.
   Glows on top."), Grim and earthy ("Browns, greys, rust and old bone. Colour is rare and
   precious.").
3. "How rough should hits and deaths be?" No blood ("Hits flash and spark. The dead fall into
   bones, dust and empty cloaks."), A little ("A splash on a hit, a stain that fades."), Lots
   ("Blood that sprays and pools, and stays on the floor.").
4. "With the dream gone, do groups of floors still take their look from old books (Frankenstein,
   Beowulf, Dracula)?" Yes, keep the books ("Each group of floors is one book's world, and the
   heroes dress for it. Just no library around it."), No, our own places ("Dungeons of our own
   making. The heroes' looks come from their gear."), Not decided yet ("Leave it open. I'll write
   rules that work either way.").

His answers:

- **Looks like:** "Dead Cells" and "Hyper Light Drifter".
- **Colour:** "Each place its own".
- **Blood:** "A little".
- **Places:** "I like the book idea for maybe generating worlds and possibly quests but I don’t
  think it needs to change the look of the heroes."

**How they were read, and told to him at 07:49:**

1. Dead Cells and Hyper Light Drifter: PIXEL ART STAYS, crisp, never smooth or plasticky. A dark
   world with bright colour in it. Neon pinks and teals (which fits his rule: cyan a friend, pink
   an enemy). Slim, stylish figures whose cloth moves. Fast, fluid animation. Dead Cells' way of
   making its art (3D figures turned into pixel art) is already how the heroes are made.
2. Each place its own colours: a dark base everywhere; each area a bold palette of its own; the
   glows on top. Added rule, told to him: NO PLACE GLOWS CYAN OR PINK, since those mean friend
   and enemy. (In the code a fire already burns "the orange of an honest fire: not the pink of an
   enemy's", `src/art/props.ts`.)
3. A little blood: a small splash on a hit, a stain that fades. Skeletons chip and crumble
   instead of bleeding.
4. Books maybe for worlds and quests, never for the heroes' looks: the heroes keep one look of
   their own, changed only by their gear; the plan of 3 Oct to dress them for each book is
   dropped (noted for the main chat at the top of this file).

He was also told: Hyper Light Drifter is spare, with few details, but on 3 Oct he asked for more
detail than the first art had (`docs/DESIGN_NOTES.md`, section 1: "the art is too basic; wants
more detail", the notes' words, not his); round 3 starts there, then movement, effects and words.

## Round 3 (asked 07:49, answered 07:56)

The questions, as put to him:

1. "How much detail? Hyper Light Drifter is spare, Dead Cells is rich." Bold and spare ("Big
   clean shapes, few small marks. Everything reads at a glance on a phone."), Rich everywhere
   ("Rivets, cracks, stitching, moss. Lots to look at up close."), Bold figures, rich places
   ("Figures clean so they pop; the world behind them full of detail."), Rich figures, quiet
   places ("Detail on heroes and monsters; the world kept simple so they stand out.").
2. "How should things move?" Fast and punchy ("Quick wind-ups, hits that land hard with a tiny
   freeze, quick recovery."), Smooth and graceful ("Flowing arcs and trailing cloth. Nothing
   jerky, a touch slower."), Slow and heavy ("Deliberate. Every swing is a commitment you can see
   coming.").
3. "How big should spells and hits be on screen?" Big and wild ("Screen-filling magic, sparks
   everywhere, the screen shakes on big hits."), Bold but clear ("Bright and punchy, but you can
   always see the fight through it."), Small and tidy ("Little flashes. The figures' moves do the
   talking.").
4. "Wordsmithing is the heart of the game. Should letters show in the world itself?" Everywhere
   ("Letters carved in walls and doors, glowing on gear, drifting off monsters that carry a
   word."), Only where a word works ("Letters glow only on things with a word in them: gear in
   hand, monsters carrying one."), Menus only ("No writing in the world. Words live in the menus
   and in what they do.").

His answers:

- **Detail:** "Bold figures, rich places".
- **Movement:** "I think this is very dependent.  And maybe this sort of thing could be asked when
  designing a new character.  I want the soldier to be heavy but quick as he’s trained with a
  sword his whole life.  But the ranger is graceful, needs to be to stay quiet in the forest"
- **Effects:** "My heart says big and wild, but my brain says bold but clear.  So maybe somewhere
  in the middle"
- **Words:** "Somewhere between 1 and 2" (1 = Everywhere, 2 = Only where a word works).

**How they were read, and told to him at 07:58:**

1. Bold figures, rich places: heroes and monsters clean and bold (big shapes, few small marks) so
   they pop; the places carry the detail (cracks, moss, carvings, clutter), kept a step quieter so
   the eye lands on the fight first.
2. MOVEMENT IS PER CHARACTER, and his idea becomes a rule: every new character gets a short brief,
   and "how does it move?" is one of its questions, answered by him. The soldier (the warrior):
   heavy but quick, a lifetime with the sword, no wasted motion, his weight behind every swing.
   The ranger: graceful and quiet, light on his feet, never a clatter. Weight stays the test for
   all of them.
3. Effects in the middle: WILD FOR AN INSTANT, THEN CLEAR. A big hit or a big spell can flare
   large, but it peaks fast and fades fast, and nothing hides the hero or a warning for more than
   a blink. Everyday hits stay bold but clean. The screen shakes only on the biggest blows.
4. Words between the two: anything with a word in it shows its letters, glowing (gear in hand, a
   monster carrying one); and the places have writing in them here and there (carved over a
   door, round a shrine) as landmarks, not on every wall.

He was told round 4 is the last before a first draft: the mage, monsters, gear and menus.
