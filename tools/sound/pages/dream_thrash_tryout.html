Dream Thrash

The first music written for Wordsmith. One tune in two halves: the dream half plays while you explore, and the thrash half punches in when a fight starts. None of it is in the game.

How to listen. Set your volume on the first clip, then leave it alone. How much louder the fight is than the exploring is part of what you are judging. Try your phone's own speaker first.

The parts on their own

Eight bars of each, turned up so you can hear it alone. If something bothers you in a clip, find it here and tell me which.

The lead

Soft for four bars, then at full voice, as in a fight.

The wash

The chords under everything, and the soft low note.

The sparkle

Small plucked notes, thrown from side to side by an echo.

The drums

Racing, a fill, then half-time.

The wall

Chords and bass: chugging, then left to ring.

The riff

Two voices, picked fast.

What I checked, since I can't hear

  • Every note is in the key, and no two voices clash. A checker reads the tune as written.
  • The strings are in tune.
  • Nothing clips and nothing clicks.
  • The fight measures about 6 units louder than the exploring, on the loudness scale that radio and streaming use. That is a clear step up.
  • A phone's speaker can't play deep notes. The kick drum, the wall and the lead each have sound in the range it does play.
  • A listening program that names what it hears was asked about each half. Its top answers for the dream half: Music, New-age music, Background music, Tender music. For the fight: Music, Video game music, Soundtrack music, Background music, Exciting music. It did not say heavy metal.

What nobody has checked

  • How it sounds. Nobody has heard this. You are the first.
  • A real phone.
  • How it sits under the game's other sounds.
  • Whether the tune comes round on itself without a seam. It is written to.
  • Bosses on the beat. Not built yet.
